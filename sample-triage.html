The research question

A queue question—not a forecast.

Domoic acid is a nerve toxin produced during some coastal algal blooms. Its laboratory test—an “assay”—costs time and money. This experiment asks whether routine measurements could help order already-collected samples for that test.

1

Start with measurements in the historical record

The model used season, chlorophyll—the green pigment used as a rough measure of algae—water temperature, the toxin value from the previous eligible panel sample and its age, plus microscope counts for two groups of Pseudo-nitzschia—a genus of microscopic algae that can produce domoic acid. The record shows these values together; it does not verify that every result was available when a real toxin-testing decision would have been made.

2

Hide one entire pier

The model learned from six piers and ranked samples at the seventh. That “whole-pier held-out” test was repeated seven times, so every displayed model score comes from a pier excluded from its own training data.

3

Reveal the historical toxin results

The slider changes the testing budget, then counts how many elevated historical samples each ranking method would have moved into the laboratory queue. It replays known outcomes; it does not predict a new one.

Recorded Lab definition

This analysis calls particulate domoic acid—the amount caught on a filter from seawater—elevated only when it is strictly greater than 0.5 micrograms per litre. It contains 180 such samples.

Official reference definition

The published California Harmful Algae Risk Mapping (“C-HARM”) product says at or above 0.5. Four otherwise eligible samples sit exactly on that boundary, so the two definitions are not identical.

Who entered the analysis: particulate domoic acid measured, at least one of the two microscope groups reported, and at least 250 eligible rows at the pier. That retained 5,328 rows at 7 of 10 toxin-measuring stations and excluded 406 toxin rows—including all three northern stations (Humboldt, Humboldt South Bay, and Trinidad) and 26 of the 206 rows above the strict Lab cutoff.

Evidence summary with release blockers

Promising history. Gated conclusion.

The model ranked historical elevated samples ahead of a strong comparison, but one safeguard failed and several release requirements are missing. The honest verdict is “GATED / WALKED-BACK,” meaning the pattern is worth studying but not cleared for operational use.

Uncertainty and variation

  • The pattern model added 0.163 AP over its baseline. Its 95% confidence interval—a resampled range showing uncertainty—was +0.073 to +0.257.
  • The simpler model added 0.224 AP. Its 95% confidence interval was +0.156 to +0.275.
  • Both beat their baselines at all 7 piers and in 11 of 13 scored years. Both were worse than baseline in their weakest year, 2018.
  • The paired score-swap checks were unlikely under random swapping, but the stricter full-refit permutation test—which re-trains the models after shuffling outcomes—was not run.
  • An earlier receipt counted 102 elevated runs by splitting after any intervening non-elevated sample. This page reports the stricter 79 gap-separated clusters and does not claim that clusters at different piers are independent.

Safeguards and missing evidence

  • False-feature controls: PASS. Random or scrambled inputs recovered at most 11.0% of the pattern-model improvement and 14.4% of the simpler-model improvement, below the recorded 50% limit.
  • Planted-signal control: FAIL overall. The pattern model added 0.0334 Average Precision, below the recorded 0.05 minimum; the simpler model added 0.0551 and passed. Both were required to pass.
  • Hypotheses attempted: NOT REPORTED. Unknown prior searching raises the chance of a lucky-looking result.
  • Calibration for probability use: NOT VALIDATED. Expected calibration error—a summary of how numerical predictions align with observed rates—was computed as 0.0196 for the pattern model and 0.0197 for its baseline, but the release evidence does not establish these scores as trustworthy probabilities. This page uses ranking only.
  • Release proof package: MISSING. There is no pre-result experiment contract, admission receipt, input-availability proof, key-shuffle proof, full-refit permutation proof, or execution-completion proof.
  • Unseen-season forward test and fresh-from-scratch release reproduction: NOT RUN. The source also has not cleared the Lab’s source review.

What this page is for

The retrospective tool is the deliverable.

A prospective pilot is not required to make this historical evaluation interactive or to support a carefully scoped research communication. It would be required before presenting the ranking as a real-time decision system.

Useful now

Explore the tradeoff between laboratory budget and elevated historical samples surfaced; explain the whole-pier evaluation; and show exactly where the evidence is promising, weak, or missing.

  • No new-sample score. The page never asks for a location or measurement and never returns a probability.
  • No bloom or safety forecast. It cannot determine whether water, wildlife, or seafood is safe.
  • Bounded analysis panel. Three northern toxin-measuring stations and 406 toxin-measured rows did not meet the recorded panel rules; this page does not claim to represent the complete statewide record.
  • No release claim. The source has not yet cleared the Lab’s source review, the prior hypothesis count is unknown, the planted-signal safeguard failed, and six required proof artifacts are missing.
  • No hidden timing assumption. “Previous toxin” means the previous eligible sample in this analysis panel, not necessarily the immediately previous assay in the full archive.
